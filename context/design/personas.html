Care Coordination — Personas

User archetypes for the Patient Triage & Readmission Risk application

Who We're Designing For

UCM's 30-day readmission problem is owned by no single team — it cuts across clinical staff in Epic and operational staff in Salesforce. These personas represent the three key user groups whose workflows must be served by the care coordination layer, ordered by design priority.

SL
Sarah Lin
Primary User
Care Coordinator, RN · Social Work background

"I know who needs help — I just can't get to everyone in time. I need to know who to call first."

Goals
  • Quickly identify which discharged patients are at highest risk of returning to the ED
  • Reach high-risk patients by phone within 48 hours of discharge
  • Log call outcomes and next steps without leaving her workflow
  • Ensure medication pickups and follow-up appointments are confirmed
Pain Points
  • Patient list is spread across Epic discharge reports and Salesforce — no single view
  • Has to manually determine call priority; no risk scoring surfaced to her
  • Call log notes entered twice — once in Epic, again in Salesforce
  • Staffing shortages mean she's managing more patients than she can realistically call
  • Suspicious of AI tools that feel like they're replacing clinical judgment
Behaviors & Context
  • Works 8–5, primarily at a desk — not bedside; Salesforce is home base
  • Union member; any workflow change requires buy-in from her and peers
  • Prefers tools that surface decisions, not tools that make them
  • Relies heavily on prior call history and social context that isn't in either system
🏥 Salesforce Health Cloud 📋 Epic (read-only) 📱 Phone / Voicemail ✉️ Marketing Cloud SMS
✓
Success Looks Like She opens the app, sees her prioritized queue, calls down the high-risk list, and logs outcomes — all in one place. No toggling between systems.
JP
James Porter
Primary User
Manager, Post-Discharge Care Coordination

"Seven high-risk patients have no one assigned to them. I need to know that before it becomes a problem — not after."

Goals
  • See team workload at a glance — who has what, who is overloaded
  • Identify and immediately assign any unowned high-risk patients
  • Track 30-day readmission rates as a leading indicator of team performance
  • Build the case for additional staffing or process changes with data
Pain Points
  • No real-time visibility into team capacity or patient assignment status
  • Readmission metrics available only in lagging reports — weekly at best
  • Has to chase coordinators for status updates rather than pulling them from a system
  • Salesforce–Epic sync delays mean the data he sees is often hours old
Behaviors & Context
  • Starts day with a team huddle; needs a dashboard that anchors that conversation
  • Escalates to CMO when readmission numbers spike — needs reliable numbers
  • Manages a unionized team; can't unilaterally change workflows without process
  • More data-literate than floor staff; comfortable with trend charts and KPIs
📊 Salesforce Reports 🏥 Health Cloud 📈 Data Cloud 📋 Epic (reporting)
✓
Success Looks Like He opens a manager dashboard, sees unassigned high-risk patients flagged at the top, assigns them in two clicks, and walks into his morning huddle with live numbers.
DR
Dr. David Reyes
Secondary User
Chief Medical Officer

"Show me something that makes me believe we can actually move the needle on readmissions — and I'll sell it upstairs."

Goals
  • Reduce UCM's 30-day readmission rate as a strategic and reputational priority
  • See proof that the investment in Salesforce tooling is generating clinical impact
  • Champion a unified patient engagement strategy to executive leadership
  • Demonstrate innovative use of AI-assisted workflows within a regulated environment
Pain Points
  • No unified view of patient engagement across marketing and operations
  • The Salesforce vs. Epic "death match" blocks meaningful progress
  • Hard to quantify the ROI of care coordination investments to board and finance
  • Frustrated that this problem "has no owner" despite broad organizational awareness
Behaviors & Context
  • Rarely in the tool day-to-day; consumes executive summary views and trend lines
  • Detail-oriented despite executive role — will probe specifics if something looks off
  • Responds strongly to visual storytelling; "flashy and innovative" resonates
  • Has significant internal sway — his buy-in can unlock organizational momentum
📊 Executive Dashboards 📈 Salesforce Analytics 📋 Slide Decks / Reports
✓
Success Looks Like He sees a compelling demo of an end-to-end patient journey — discharge to safe at-home — with a clear risk reduction narrative he can bring to the board.
