Where the gains came from

The largest absolute reduction is in the high-risk tier — precisely the population coordinators were previously least able to prioritize. This is the strongest available evidence that triage ordering, not additional staffing, drove the improvement.

Known Limitation

Social determinants data remains sparse. The risk model under-weights housing instability and food insecurity — factors disproportionately affecting our South Side patient population.

What Actually Changed
The program shifted from reactive work to an evidence-based transition bundle: early outreach, medication reconciliation, follow-up scheduling, and social barrier resolution.
Before — Two Systems, No Bridge
Epic EHR
Knows who was discharged, their diagnosis, their medications, their prior admissions. Does not know if anyone called them.
✕  no shared view  ✕
Salesforce
Knows who was called, who opted into SMS, who has a coordinator assigned. Does not know who is clinically at risk.
Coordinators reconciled the two by hand — reading an Epic discharge report on one screen, searching Salesforce on another. Prioritization was memory and guesswork.

Care Coordination Layer
Reads both · resolves conflicts · scores risk · routes to the right coordinator
One prioritized list. Every value carries its origin. Conflicts between the systems are resolved by explicit rule and shown to the coordinator — not hidden.
Why this matters strategically
We did not have to win the Epic-versus-Salesforce argument to make progress. Both systems keep their territory. The orchestration layer sits above them and is the only place that needs to understand both — which means this approach is replicable for any other cross-system problem we face.
